# Trade carts and land trade

Status: planned

## Design

Round: roadmap P1 item 5's last leftover — trade carts and land trade, which closes item 5.
The bot leads the project: work the roadmap in order and run each round without waiting for
approval. The design below is approved as written.

**What you'll see:** the Market gains a trainable unit — a `cart` (Trade Cart) drawn with cart
frames — and the market's command grid gets a Train button like the barracks' Train Militia.
Train one, select it, right-click a second own market: the cart shuttles between the two
markets and every completed delivery pays gold into the treasury, with the route shown on the
selected cart's panel (`Trading A ↔ B, 6g per trip`). Nothing else in the village changes.

**How it works:** the cart joins `data/units.json` as a vendored row — `id: 128`,
`internalName: "TCART"` — with that row's numbers copied, not retuned (hp 70, attack 0,
speed 1.25 tiles/s, lineOfSight 7, trainTime 51, cost 100 wood + 50 gold). `label`, `r`,
`buildable` and `atkType` are authored fields per the file's `authored[]` rule; the row carries
no `Attacks`, so it attacks nobody. Two authored `cfg` numbers come with it and are listed in
`design{}` because the table carries no trade rule: `TRADE_GOLD_TILES: 8` (one gold per 8
tiles of route) and `TRADE_GOLD_MAX: 10`. `data/buildings.json` gains one authored change — the
market's `trains` becomes `["cart"]`, `trains` already being an authored column — and
`src/hud.js` draws the button through the existing per-building train path; `trainUnit` needs
no new rule beyond the row existing. Land trade is one new order kind:
`setTradeRoute(Game, unitId, buildingId)` in section 4, validated (own unit, a `cart`, a
finished friendly market that is not the one it stands on) and otherwise setting
`u.order = { kind: 'trade', from, to }`; `sim.updateUnit`'s order switch gains a `trade` case
that walks the cart to the target market's standing spot, on arrival pays
`min(TRADE_GOLD_MAX, max(1, floor(tiles / TRADE_GOLD_TILES)))` gold, swaps `from`/`to` and
keeps shuttling — no second timer, the same shape the `harvest` and `build` orders already use.
The cart's other rules come free from the garrison round: it can be garrisoned in its market
(capacity 10), it takes no work spot, and the enemy AI neither trains nor moves it.
`assets/sprites.png` gains the cart's cells and nothing else: four frames (`idle`/`walk` ×
blue/red) authored procedurally as `data/sprites/units/cart/*.png` — the same class of change
the farm cell set, which `data/README.md` documents as a procedural cell vendored as a PNG —
declared in `data/sprites.json`, with `assets/sprites.png` regenerated by `tools/gen-atlas.js`
in the same commit as the manifest edit and staged by name, never hand-edited. `CFG.VERSION`
stays at 2: the garrison round's precedent is that new rows and new unit fields ride the
copy-every-key `encodeUnit`/`encodeBuilding` contract, so the save payload shape is unchanged.

**Not in this round:** the enemy AI trading or training carts, trade posts and forts as route
ends, water trade, cart combat or cart upgrades, the market's existing sell/buy 100-unit lots
(unchanged — a cart route neither consumes nor replaces them), and ripped AoE2 cart art (the
roadmap's asset-source work stays separate; this round adds only the four procedural frames the
atlas needs).

**How we check it:** `tools/checks/06-training-research-trade.js` gains a cart section written
before the code: `trainUnit(market, 'cart')` spends exactly 100 wood + 50 gold and respects the
market's `trains` list; `setTradeRoute` refuses a non-cart unit, an enemy market and an
unfinished market with `Game.res` untouched and accepts a valid pair; a cart arriving at the
far market pays the exact `floor(tiles / 8)` gold capped at 10 and reverses its route — proven
once at a measured distance inside the cap and once past it. `tools/checks/02-sprites.js`
proves the cart's four cells exist, are unique in the sheet and match `spriteFor('cart', …)`;
that is the check that must fail the moment a kind is added without art, so it lands in the same
commit as the frames. `tools/checks/09-hud.js` proves the market grid shows the Train button and
a selected cart's panel names its route and its per-trip gold.
`tools/checks/10-reset-save.js` proves a cart mid-route survives save/load with its order
(`encodeOrder` gaining the `from`/`to` pair). `tools/check-stats.mjs` proves `src/config.js`
carries the row and the two cfg numbers byte-for-byte; `node tools/headless-check.js -q` and
`node tools/render-preview.js` stay green with `preview.png` regenerated and staged.

Seven places where the design's prose and the current tree disagree or leave a hole, settled
here so the executor does not have to guess:

- **`trainUnit` is `train`.** The trainable path is `train(Game, bld, kind)`
  (`src/commands.js:218` → `:224` after T3's insert); it needs no new rule, only the row and
  the market's `trains` entry, exactly as the design says. Every command below names it as
  `train`.
- **`encodeOrder` already copies the `from`/`to` pair.** `encodeOrder` (`src/save.js:47`)
  copies *every* key of the order and rewrites only `src`, so a
  `{kind:'trade', from, to}` order is saved whole with no edit to `src/save.js` — the design's
  "`encodeOrder` gaining the `from`/`to` pair" is a claim about the current file, and
  T5's two checks are what prove it (the garrison plan's T4 is the same shape: checks, no
  `src/save.js` change, `CFG.VERSION` stays 2). `decodeOrder` (`src/save.js:173`) returns the
  plain copy for any order without a `src` object, so nothing needs a new branch either.
- **The `buildingId` argument is the market object, and `from` is computed, not passed.**
  `garrisonUnits(Game, unitIds, b)` and `train(Game, bld, kind)` both take the building object,
  so `setTradeRoute(Game, unitId, b)` does too (the design's `buildingId` names the market, not
  a number). The design's "the market it stands on" has to be *derived*, because a unit carries
  no home-building field and a trained cart is placed on the market's standing ring
  (`sim.js:14`'s `workPos(Game, b, null)` = the ring's first tile), never on its footprint.
  Settled, one expression inside `setTradeRoute`: the cart's own-side finished Market he is
  closest to by `Math.hypot(b.x - u.x, b.y - u.y)` — the `findDropoff` scoring shape
  (`src/economy.js:31-44`) — preferring `u.garrisonedIn`'s building when he is inside one, so
  the garrison round's cart and a freshly trained one resolve the same way. `to === from` is the
  design's refusal (`'Already trading with that market'`); the pair it writes is then
  `{kind:'trade', from: from.id, to: b.id}`.
- **The gold number needs one owner.** The design puts the formula in `sim.updateUnit` and the
  same number on the cart's panel, so it lives in one exported pure helper next to the command —
  `tradeGoldFor(Game, a, b)` — with `tiles = Math.hypot(a.x - b.x, a.y - b.y) / CFG.TILE`, the
  straight-line tile distance between the two markets (not the walked route, which changes with
  the cart's position), and the body `Math.min(CFG.TRADE_GOLD_MAX, Math.max(1,
  Math.floor(tiles / CFG.TRADE_GOLD_TILES)))`. The `popCommitted` precedent (`src/economy.js:118`,
  "the single helper `train` and the footer's button both consult, so a command and its button
  cannot disagree") is the pattern; the sim pays what the helper says and the panel prints what
  the helper says. Because the value is derived from the two buildings and never stored, a save
  cannot carry a stale number.
- **The data row and its art are one commit, not two.** `tools/gen-atlas.js:141-146` fails on
  `KINDS.cart` with no manifest entry *and* on a manifest kind `KINDS` lacks, and
  `tools/checks/02-sprites.js:19-44` fails on a kind with no cells — so the row, the four PNGs
  and both generated blocks land together (the design already demands the atlas be committed
  with the manifest). T1 is that commit; the design's `train(market,'cart')` proof could not
  exist before it.
- **The other seven columns of the row.** `check-stats.mjs:57-79` (`kindValue`) demands
  `atkTime`, `meleeArmor`, `pierceArmor`, `range`, `minRange`, `reload`, `accuracy` on every
  row, and the design names only five numbers. They come off the same vendored row
  (`data.Unit[128]`, re-read against the upstream table this session): `atkTime: 0`
  (`AttackDelaySeconds`), `meleeArmor: 0`, `pierceArmor: 0`, `range: 0`, `minRange: 0`,
  `reload: 0` (`ReloadTime`), `accuracy: 0` (`AccuracyPercent`) — copied, not retuned, and not
  `design{}` entries (unlike villager/militia `atkTime`, the table does carry them). The two
  `cfg` numbers the design does put in `design{}` are added there verbatim, plus `cfgNotes`
  lines so the generated `CFG` keeps a comment per key.
- **A cart with `atk: 0` would still swing, and `unitAnim` would never show its walk frames.**
  `hitUnit` deals `Math.max(1, dmg - armour)` (`src/sim.js:81`) and the attack branch sets
  `u.cd = u.atkTime` (`src/sim.js:234`) — with the row's `atkTime: 0` an idle cart that
  auto-acquired a fight would deal 1 damage every frame. The design's "it attacks nobody" is
  therefore a code rule, not a data fact: `autoAcquire` gains the sibling of the rule it already
  has on line 170 (`if (u.kind === 'villager') return null;`, and `stepBuildingAttacks:138`
  `!BLD[b.kind].attack` for buildings) — `if (u.kind === 'villager' || u.kind === 'cart')
  return null;`. It does not touch the `attack` order an explicit right-click can still give a
  cart (villagers keep the same behaviour today). Second half: `unitAnim`
  (`src/render.js:140-154`) answers `idle` for any order kind it does not know, so the cart's
  two new `walk` cells would be dead art; the switch's walk case becomes
  `case 'move': case 'go_deposit': case 'trade': return 'walk';` (+1 line), which is what
  `tools/checks/02-sprites.js` §5b2 and `src/render.js:156`/`:246` doc refs then cover.
